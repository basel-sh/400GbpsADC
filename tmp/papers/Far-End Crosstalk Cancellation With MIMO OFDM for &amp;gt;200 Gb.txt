Far-End Crosstalk Cancellation With MIMO OFDM
for >200 Gb/s ADC-Based Serial Links
Gain Kim
, Member, IEEE
Abstract—This brief presents an area- and power-efﬁcient far-
end crosstalk (FEXT) cancellation scheme with multi-input multi-
output
(MIMO)
orthogonal
frequency-division
multiplexing
(OFDM) for an analog-to-digital converter (ADC)-based ultra-
high-speed serial links. The frequency-domain per-sub-channel
equalization nature of OFDM allows a simple FEXT cancella-
tion with MIMO equalizer in its digital signal processor (DSP),
unlike time-domain pulse amplitude modulation (PAM) that
requires heavy parallel cross-lane ﬁltering for FEXT cancellation.
With architectural modeling of a 2 × 2 MIMO OFDM wire-
line transceiver (TRX), simulation results show that the main
and FEXT channels’ frequency responses are properly estimated
using pilot symbols. With the estimated channel responses, simu-
lation results also show that the FEXT can be effectively canceled
out with per-sub-channel MIMO equalization, achieving an over-
all symbol error rate (SER) of 1.4 × 10−5 at 224 Gb/s/lane for a
channel having 23 dB loss at 40 GHz, while the SER is 4 × 10−1
without FEXT cancellation.
Index Terms—Serial link, wireline transceiver, ADC-based
serial link, orthogonal frequency division multiplexing, OFDM,
wireline communications, wireline receiver, far-end crosstalk,
FEXT.
I. INTRODUCTION
T
HE INCREASING demands for ever-higher communi-
cation bandwidth for cloud computing platforms and
data centers have been leading the per-lane data rate (DR)
increase of wireline transceivers (TRX) from 28 Gb/s [1] to
112 Gb/s [2], [3] for the last decade. While non-return-to-
zero (NRZ) was the dominating modulation for DR < 56 Gb/s
with analog building blocks for its TRX, 4-level pulse ampli-
tude modulation (PAM-4) with a high-speed analog-to-digital
converter (ADC) and digital signal processor (DSP)-based
receiver (RX) became popular for DR ≥56 Gb/s [4], [5], [6],
[7], [8]. Digital-to-analog converter (DAC)-based transmitter
(TX) is widely used for DR ≥112 Gb/s together with the
ADC-based RX providing high ﬂexibility and strong equal-
ization capabilities [9], [10]. While the PAM-4 TRX with a
DAC-based TX and ADC-based RX has kept pushing the per-
lane communication speed, its extension to > 200 Gb/s for

Moreover, handling the increasing far-end crosstalk (FEXT)
typically relies on keeping a sufﬁcient link margin, instead of
canceling the FEXT [2].
For single-ended dense interconnects at a relatively low
speed (7 Gb/s/lane), analog cross-equalizers including both
continuous-time and decision-feedback equalizers between
lanes are introduced in [11] for FEXT cancellation. However,
analog cross-equalizers have limitations for being employed
for DR > 200 Gb/s due to the bandwidth requirements and
stringent timing constraints, and ADC-based PAM-4 cross
equalizers will be excessively heavy in terms of power and area
due to the required parallel long-tap ﬁnite impulse response
(FIR) ﬁlters.
Employing
orthogonal
frequency-division
multiplexing
(OFDM) which is a frequency-domain modulation, time-
domain convolution (ﬁltering) operation reduces to element-
wise multiplication for the equalization process, simplifying
the RX equalizer DSP [12]. Demonstrated with a 56 Gb/s
silicon prototype [12], extending OFDM for > 100 Gb/s
serial links has been studied in [13], [14]. Beneﬁting from
the per-sub-channel frequency-domain equalization nature
of OFDM, this brief presents an area- and power-efﬁcient
FEXT cancellation for ultra-high-speed ADC-based serial
links with multi-input multi-output (MIMO) OFDM RX
equalizer.
The rest of this brief is organized as follows. The overall
architecture of a wireline OFDM TRX is brieﬂy reviewed in
Section II. Section III explains the FEXT cancellation scheme
with a 2 × 2 MIMO OFDM and the MIMO equalizer coefﬁ-
cient calculation. Section IV explains the 2×2 MIMO channel
estimation ﬂow. Simulation results are shown in Section V, and
Section VI concludes this brief.

with the same target data rate, excluding the resources related
to the fast Fourier transform (FFT) that efﬁciently implements
the DFT.

Once the TX and the RX are synchronized, pilot symbols are
sent for the RX channel estimation (CHEST). The CHEST is
repeated every transmission of M OFDM symbols; hence the
effective data rate degradation is 100× 1
M %, which is negligible
for a sufﬁciently large M such as M > 100.

VI. CONCLUSION
Area- and energy-efﬁcient FEXT cancellation scheme with
MIMO OFDM for ultra-high-speed electrical serial links is
presented. The MIMO OFDM TRX with the frequency-
domain per-sub-channel equalization nature effectively cancels
out the FEXT with a lightweight DSP overhead. The cost
to pay is only a single complex multiplier per sub-channel
per aggressor, while time-domain modulations such as PAM-4
or PAM-8 with ADC-based RX require heavy parallel con-
volution units per aggressor that are considerably heavy in
terms of area and power. Simulation results with one aggres-
sor demonstrate that the MIMO OFDM can be effective for
> 200 Gb/s/lane dense electrical links beyond PAM.
